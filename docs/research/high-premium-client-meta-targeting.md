# Find SortMyCover's R1,500 breadwinner on Meta

The ideal R1,500+/month SortMyCover prospect is a **salaried professional, manager or growing business owner aged roughly 35–50, earning about R35,000–R55,000+ gross a month (or a dual-income household of R50,000–R100,000), with a bond, children and medical aid, living mainly in the Johannesburg north / Tshwane east / Cape Town–Stellenbosch belt**. At that premium the person is buying multi-million-rand protection, around **R6.5m of life-only cover at age 45** on the one insurer illustration found, or a smaller life amount bundled with disability and severe-illness benefits. The pool is small: around **1 million South Africans earned over R500,000 a year** in the latest band data found, against about **34 million Facebook users**. Meta will not find these people for you through targeting. South Africa has no income targeting, interest targeting under Advantage+ only "suggests" an audience, and Meta's personal-attributes rule bars copy that tells the viewer they are affluent. The levers that do work are **hard geography, a minimum age, exclusions, creative that describes a situation in the third person, a Higher Intent form that splits the budget question at R1,500, and the Qualified/GoodFit signal fed back to Meta**. Cycle 1 should run as one Advantage+ leads campaign at about R350/day on those levers. Test through creative concepts and one form split, judged on cost per verified qualified lead and on the share of verified leads at R1,500+, not on cost per lead (CPL). Read every figure here with one limitation in mind: **the researchers could not open any primary page** (Meta, SARS, Stats SA, ASISA, insurers, BusinessTech and others were blocked by the network proxy). Every number comes from search-result summaries, so most evidence is Grade C and several key items have to be confirmed in Ads Manager before launch.

**How to read the evidence grades.** A = regulator, official or Meta's own documentation. B = independent study or reputable secondary source. C = media, vendor or practitioner claim. D = assumption or inference. Where an official figure reached us only through a media summary, it is graded C. Anything marked **ASSUMPTION** is an inference drawn for this report and is not backed by a source.

## R1,500 a month buys millions in cover and implies R35k–R55k gross income

No insurer quote table by age could be retrieved, so the picture rests on two anchors. A 1Life broker document, seen only as a search summary, shows **a 45-year-old paying about R1,400/month getting a recommended R6.5 million in life cover** ([1Life broker PDF, March 2024](https://1life.co.za/globalassets/broker/resources/2024/2403---1life-life-cover.pdf); Grade C, assumptions about smoker status and health not visible). At the low end, roughly **R400/month buys about R750,000** ([1Life blog](https://www.1life.co.za/blog/what-you-should-know-before-buying-life-insurance-in-south-africa); Grade C). Premiums rise with age, poorer health, smoking and riskier occupations ([Dial Direct](https://dialdirect.co.za/blog/general-news/factors-that-influence-your-life-cover-monthly-premiums/); Grade C). The directional reading (**ASSUMPTION**) is that R1,500 buys more life cover at 35, materially less at 50, and much less life cover once disability, severe-illness and income-protection benefits are bundled in, because living benefits cost far more per rand of cover. In practice, a R1,500+ client is buying a breadwinner's full protection stack, not entry-level term or funeral cover.

Income follows from that. The only percentage-of-income rule found says **yearly risk premiums should stay under 5–6% of annual income**, but that source is Indian, not South African ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pf/planning-to-buy-term-insurance-restrict-premium-to-5-of-annual-income-119090901358_1.html); Grade D for SA use). On that rule, R18,000 a year in premiums implies **R300,000–R360,000 gross a year (R25,000–R30,000/month) as the floor**. A second check agrees with it. South African guides suggest cover of **six to ten times gross annual income** ([1Life](https://www.1life.co.za/blog/the-best-life-cover-in-sa); [Cape Argus](https://capeargus.co.za/business/advice/2024-04-18-this-is-how-you-can-calculate-how-much-life-cover-you-need/); Grade C/D). Working back from R6.5m at roughly 10x gives about R650,000 a year, or R54,000/month. A worked example (**ASSUMPTION**): a R40,000/month earner at 10x needs R4.8m, plus a R1–2m bond and R0.5–1m for education, which comes to about R6–8m and is consistent with the R1,400 illustration. **The comfortable band is therefore about R35,000–R55,000 gross a month per individual.** A household with two earners on R25,000–R35,000 each also qualifies. That second group matters, because their Meta profiles will not look affluent.

The pool is narrow and is not growing at the top. In 2020/21 about **1 million people (6.7% of the working population) had taxable income above R500,000 (R42,000/month)**, about 462,000 were above R750,000, and about 242,000 were above R1m ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/616167/this-is-how-many-people-in-south-africa-earn-more-than-r42000-a-month/); Grade C, dated). More recent reporting puts **569,351 taxpayers above R1m**, up 16% ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/816827/how-many-millionaires-pay-tax-in-south-africa/); Grade C). That article expresses the figure as 3.94% of 14.45m registered taxpayers, while SARS's own 2025 statistics cite 26.16m registered individuals and 7.71m assessed taxpayers ([SARS 2025 Tax Statistics](https://www.sars.gov.za/2025-tax-statistics-pdf/); Grade A via summary). The bases differ and the year labels look inconsistent, so treat the share as indicative only. Average take-home pay was about **R18,098 in January 2025** ([IOL/BankservAfrica](https://iol.co.za/business/jobs/2025-02-26-south-african-salaries-surge-take-home-pay-hits-r18098-in-positive-economic-shift/); Grade B/C), far below the target. BankservAfrica data also shows **salaries in the R40,000–R100,000 band shrinking** while growth sits under R10,000 and at R20,000–R30,000 ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/835516/trouble-for-south-africans-earning-more-than-r40000-a-month/); Grade C). The **ASSUMPTION** is that people earning R300,000+ number roughly 1.5–2.5 million. In other words, the R1,500+ buyer is in the top tenth or so of earners, and that tier is under pressure, not expanding.

The need is real even at the top, and that gives creative its most defensible message. ASISA's 2025 gap study puts the **life and disability shortfall at R50.4 trillion**, with the average earner needing **R2.1m of life cover but holding R800,000** ([ASISA](https://www.asisa.org.za/media-releases/south-africa-s-life-and-disability-insurance-shortfall-widens-to-r504-trillion/); Grade A/B). **Earners above R30,000/month hold only about 49% of the life cover they need** ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/affordability-pressures-to-drive-innovation-as-insurance-gap-hits-r50-trillion/); Grade C). Old Mutual says that group is short by more than **R3m in life cover and R5m in disability cover** ([Cover.co.za podcast](https://www.cover.co.za/podcasts/why-south-africas-protection-gap-keeps-widening---a-conversation-on-underinsurance-and-the-value-of-advice-with-arno-jansen-van-vuuren); Grade C). Of 10.8m new risk policies written in 2025, only **2.7m were life, disability, critical-illness or income-protection policies**; the rest were funeral and credit life ([IOL/ASISA](https://iol.co.za/personal-finance/financial-planning/2026-04-04-asisa-reports-r626-billion-in-life-insurance-claims-paid-in-2025/); Grade A via media). The R1,500+ buyer comes from a subset of that 2.7m.

## Bonded, medical-aid households in Johannesburg, Tshwane and the Western Cape

The household markers are clear even if their exact sizes are not. **Only 6.6% of South African households are still paying off a bond** (Census 2022, via [Daily News](https://dailynews.co.za/news/politics/2023-10-11-gauteng-residents-are-more-likely-to-rent-than-own-their-homes--census-2022/); Grade A via media), so having a bond is itself a filter for this segment. ooba's 2025 data puts **first-time buyers at an average age of 35 with R1.1m bonds**, and the all-buyer average purchase price just under R1.7m ([BusinessTech/ooba](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/833193/great-news-for-anyone-looking-to-buy-a-home-in-south-africa/); Grade C). Western Cape buyers average **R2.39m** ([DailyInvestor](https://dailyinvestor.com/property/106515/the-cheapest-and-most-expensive-provinces-to-buy-property-in-south-africa/); Grade C). Standard Bank reports first-time buyers averaging 38, with **self-employed bond registrations up 33%** ([Standard Bank](https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/news-and-media/newsroom/sa-changing-homebuyer-market-more-women-younger-buyers-rise-in-self-employed-first-time-homeowners); Grade C). Self-employed owners matter because they lack employer group cover. Medical-aid membership, the best available proxy for affluence, is highest in the **Western Cape (25.4%) and Gauteng (21.3%)** and lowest in Limpopo and KZN (about 10%) ([Stats SA](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=19353); Grade A via summary). Private schooling is a minority signal: about 5.8% of primary enrolment, with average fees near R125,000 a year ([Trading Economics/World Bank](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/school-enrollment-primary-private-percent-of-total-primary-wb-data.html); [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/lifestyle/804661/d-most-expensive-private-schools-in-south-africa-in-2025-with-one-now-over-r400000-a-year-wip/); Grade B/C).

Geography can only be ranked with evidence at municipality level. SARS-derived 2023 averages put **City of Johannesburg at R484,671 average taxable income, Stellenbosch R458,198, Tshwane R413,632, and Midvaal in the top six** ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/804100/the-tiny-municipality-in-south-africa-where-people-earn-more-than-r500000-a-year/); Grade C). The resulting tiers are below. Suburb names inside each node are **ASSUMPTIONS** taken from the brief and property data, not from income evidence.

Two caveats apply. First, ultra-luxury suburbs (Llandudno, Sandhurst, Westcliff, with averages of R9–24m per [DailyInvestor/Lightstone](https://dailyinvestor.com/property/116242/the-small-suburb-selling-the-most-expensive-homes-in-south-africa/)) sit above this segment and are probably already served by private-wealth advisers. The volume is in upper-middle estates and suburbs (**ASSUMPTION**). Second, the life-event story behind "new bond, new baby, new spouse" is weaker than marketers assume. An Olico study of more than 10,000 direct-marketed applicants found the **typical direct buyer was unmarried, childless and not a homeowner** ([Cover.co.za](https://www.cover.co.za/news/unlocking-the-true-value-of-life-cover); Grade C), and LIMRA found marriage or parenthood prompts only a minority of purchases ([LIMRA](https://limra.com/en/newsroom/industry-trends/2013/myth-becoming-a-parent-or-getting-married-prompts-the-majority-of-people-to-shop-and-buy-life-insurance); Grade B, US, 2013). Read together, the two findings suggest that people who answer direct-response ads are not automatically the bonded breadwinner. If SortMyCover wants that person, the funnel has to filter actively for them.

Reach is not the constraint. NapoleonCat counts **34.3m Facebook users in SA, 20.4% aged 35–44 and 9.3% aged 45–54**, and **9.4m Instagram users, 15.4% and 7.4%** in the same bands ([NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/social-media-users-in-south_africa/2025); Grade C, based on ad-audience counts). Simple arithmetic gives roughly 10m Facebook and 2m Instagram users aged 35–54, which are ceilings, not real people. Facebook carries most of this segment. Instagram skews younger and more urban.

## Meta's levers in SA: geography, age floor, exclusions and the feedback signal

**Special Ad Category is the first unknown.** Meta's "Financial Products and Services" category explicitly covers insurance. Its mandatory use from 14 January 2025 is documented for **advertisers in the US or ads shown to US audiences**, and one summary also mentions Canada and parts of Europe ([Meta Marketing API docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category); [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/); Grade A via snippet). No source found says South Africa is in scope. If the category is declared or forced, the reported restrictions are severe: **age locked to about 18–65+, gender locked to all, a minimum location radius of about 15 miles (25 km), no location exclusions, and restricted lookalike and saved audiences** ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta/meta-special-ad-category-requirements/financial-products-and-services-special-ad-category/); [Data Axle](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/); Grade B/C, US-centric). Those rules would remove the age floor and the tight geography this report relies on. Do not declare the category unless Ads Manager prompts for it, and settle this before launch. If Ads Manager does prompt for it, the plan falls back to creative, form and feedback levers only.

**Detailed targeting is now a suggestion, not a filter.** Detailed-targeting exclusions were removed from 31 March 2025. Interests were merged into broader groups from 23 June 2025, and affected legacy campaigns had to switch by 15 January 2026 ([Conversios](https://www.conversios.io/blog/meta-advantage-audience-vs-detailed-targeting-2026-guide/); Grade C). Inside Advantage+ Audience, **only location, minimum age and custom-audience exclusions act as hard constraints**. Everything else is a hint the system can expand beyond ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/metas-advantage-audience/); Grade C). Practitioners describe the Andromeda retrieval system as matching on **creative relevance and predicted outcomes rather than audience settings**, and recommend one campaign, one broad ad set and many diverse creatives ([EasyInsights](https://easyinsights.ai/blog/lead-gen-shifts-profitable-meta-ads-2026/); [PPC Hero](https://ppchero.com/?p=31064); Grade C). **Household-income targeting exists in the US and reportedly in India, with no sign of it in SA** ([Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/news/meta-launches-household-income-targeting-in-india); Grade C). Meta removed health, race, religion and political targeting options in 2022 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/392609/meta-to-remove-more-detailed-targeting-options-dee.html); Grade C). Candidate affluence and life-stage suggestions, such as small-business owners, page admins, frequent travellers, recently moved, new parents and golf, may or may not still exist for SA. Each one has to be searched for in Ads Manager.

**Geography is the strongest affluence lever that can actually be set.** Meta generally supports region, city, postcode and pin-plus-radius targeting, with custom radii of **1–80 km** and up to 50,000 postcodes ([Big Eye Agency](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/geographic-targeting-for-meta-ads-guide-2026); [Meta basic targeting reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/reference/basic-targeting); Grade C, not SA-specific). Whether SA postcodes and suburbs resolve cleanly has not been verified.

**Quality feedback is the best-documented lever, and cycle 1 probably cannot use the native version.** Meta's Conversion Leads (Conversions API for CRM) optimises toward a CRM stage, and Meta says it "may yield higher quality leads". It needs **about 200+ leads a month, uploads at least daily, the Meta lead ID stored and returned with each event, and an optimised stage that happens within 28 days and converts 1–40% of leads** ([Meta CAPI for CRM docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/conversion-leads-integration); Grade A via snippet). At R350/day (about R10,500 per cycle), 200 leads means a CPL of about **R52 or less**. A Higher Intent, filtered form is unlikely to hit that. So the Qualified/GoodFit offline events should still be sent from day one, because they build history and seed audiences, but expect Meta to optimise on the lead event until eligibility is reached (**ASSUMPTION** on CPL). The 1–40% rule also matters for a R1,500+ "GoodFit" stage: if fewer than 1% of leads reach it, it cannot be the optimisation event. Lookalikes need **at least 100 people from one country**, and 1,000–5,000 is more stable ([Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/why-facebook-lookalike-audiences-are-worth-your-ad-spend); Grade C). Value-based optimisation for the Leads objective was not confirmed.

**What the policies forbid.** Meta's personal-attributes standard bars ads that **assert or imply personal attributes, including vulnerable financial status, family status and age, or that imply knowledge of a person's financial information** ([Meta Transparency Centre](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes); Grade B via summary). "You" and "your" are allowed as long as they do not point at such an attribute; "Are you in debt?" is the standard example of a violation ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/can-we-use-the-words-you-and-your-in-facebook-ads-copy/); Grade B/C). Meta's financial-services standard also requires local licensing compliance, but its text could not be read ([Meta financial services policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/financial-services)). On the South African side, **FAIS General Code of Conduct s14 (amended 2020) requires ads to be factually correct and balanced, and to carry an FSP licence reference, with "advertisement" defined broadly** ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/amending-the-rules-of-advertising-how-to-keep-your-ads-safe-and-within-legal-limits/); Grade B). The ARB Code requires documentary substantiation of factual claims (cl 4.1) and bans misleading implication (cl 4.2.1) ([Code of Advertising Practice](https://www.autotrader.co.za/Common/Views/Informational/CODE%20OF%20ADVERTISING%20PRACTICE%20v2021.pdf); Grade B). One question goes to `needs_human`: **whose FSP number appears on a broker-neutral SortMyCover ad**. In `named` consent mode with one broker it is presumably Mark's, but that is a legal decision, not a research finding.

## Creative and form do the filtering, as long as copy describes the situation

With targeting this blunt, **the creative and the form are the affluence filter**. Practitioners call ad copy a filter that should name the customer type and the commitment level ([clicksgeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-not-generating-quality-leads/); Grade C), but their US example names an asset band, which in SA would collide with Meta's personal-attributes rule and FAIS balance requirements. The safe version **describes the situation the adviser helps with, in the third person, and never describes the viewer**.

| Copy pattern | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Many South African earners carry less than half the cover their families would need" (cite ASISA) | Safer, needs substantiation file | Third person and factual (ARB 4.1); no viewer attribute |
| "A free conversation with an independent adviser about protecting a bond, school fees and a family's income" | Safer | Describes the service and the situation; no product or premium |
| "For business partners and practice owners thinking about what happens if one partner can't work" | Safer, not tested (D) | Self-selects owners with no assertion about the viewer; avoid naming buy-and-sell or key-person *products* |
| "Earning over R50k a month?" / "You have a bond and a growing family" | **Breach** | Second-person financial or family status (Meta personal attributes) |
| "Best for households earning R50k+" | **High risk** | States an income gate; may imply attributes and mislead (ARB 4.2.1) |
| "From R1,500 a month", any insurer or product name, "you need R5m cover" | **Breach** | Premium, product or advice under FAIS no-advice positioning |

Imagery follows the same rule (**ASSUMPTION**, Grade D): professional settings such as a home office, a consulting room or a kitchen table with documents, and family-at-home scenes in upper-middle homes, rather than displays of wealth or anything that suggests the viewer's status. Health and illness imagery should be avoided (sensitive-attribute risk). Supported hooks are the shortfall statistic, "protecting the bond and school fees", and owners with no employer cover. An Ad Library review was not possible from the research environment. A manual pass (SA, active ads, terms such as "financial planning", "key person", "estate", sorted by run length) is the cheapest way to see what has lasted.

**The form split is the single most important change.** Meta's Higher Intent form adds a review screen. It reportedly **cuts volume by 20–40% in exchange for cleaner leads** ([Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-capture-more-qualified-leads-with-facebook-ads/); [Straight North](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/lead-generation-on-meta-9-proven-tactics-for-delivering-high-quality-leads/); Grade C). One or two qualifying questions such as budget filter out the curious ([adsuploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/facebook-lead-ads); Grade C). Life-insurance practitioners say this raises CPL and lowers cost per sale ([megadigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/facebook-ads-for-life-insurance/); Grade C). No controlled or SA-specific test was found. To keep the canonical bands intact, **split only the top band**:

| Form option (monthly amount the person would be comfortable setting aside) | Qualifies (canonical) | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Under R750 | Per existing rules | `budget_low` |
| R750–R1,250 | Yes | `budget_core` |
| R1,250–R1,499 | Yes | `budget_upper` |
| R1,500 or more | Yes | `budget_1500plus`, priority routing |

Ask about a **comfortable monthly amount, not income**. It is less intrusive, it is a choice rather than a condition, and the form never shows it as a price (**ASSUMPTION**; Meta's instant-form rules on financial questions and the POPIA consent wording could not be read and must be checked). Household income, if needed at all, belongs later in the WhatsApp conversation. The R1,500+ tag should drive broker priority and become a **GoodFit** signal (Qualified + verified + R1,500+). That gives Meta a richer target without rejecting anyone.

## Cycle 1: one Advantage+ campaign at R350/day, tested through creative

The setup below follows the canonical one-campaign structure and the pre-mortem rule of no panic changes before 14 days unless an SLO burns. Items marked VERIFY depend on the Ads Manager checks at the end.

| Setting | Recommendation | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign | One Advantage+ leads campaign, about R350/day (about R10,500/cycle), **no Special Ad Category unless prompted** (VERIFY) | Meta scope docs (A snippet) |
| Location (hard) | Tier 1 only: City of Johannesburg, Tshwane, Midvaal/Ekurhuleni east, City of Cape Town, Stellenbosch/Helderberg; "people living in" (VERIFY). Durban/Umhlanga added in cycle 2 only if Tier 1 saturates | SARS municipal income, medical aid (C) |
| Age | Minimum age 30 as the hard floor; suggestion 35–55. Form bands stay <35/35–44/45–50/51+ | Need and affordability peak 35–50 (C/D) |
| Audience hints | Optional: small-business owners, recently moved, parents (if still listed), personal finance. Treat as suggestions | C/D |
| Exclusions | Custom audiences: all prior leads, existing clients, disqualified and unverified leads | Hard constraint (C) |
| Placements | Advantage+ placements minus Audience Network (VERIFY possible) | C/D |
| Form | Higher Intent; budget question split at R1,500; WhatsApp verification within 72 h as canonical | C |
| Signals | CAPI offline events from day 1: `Qualified` (verified), `GoodFit` (verified + R1,500+). Optimise on leads until Conversion Leads eligibility, then on `Qualified` | Meta CAPI for CRM (A snippet) |
| Creative | 3 concepts × 2–3 executions each (6–9 ads): (a) family shortfall / bond and school fees, (b) business owner or practice partner, (c) broad "talk to an independent adviser". Code-rendered stills and Reels | Andromeda creative-led delivery (C) |

**How to test.** Splitting the budget across ad sets would starve each one. Most of the test therefore happens inside the single campaign, by comparing creative concepts on downstream outcomes. Each lead carries its ad ID into the CRM, and each concept is judged on **cost per verified qualified lead, share of verified leads at R1,500+, and cost per verified R1,500+ lead**. The one structural test worth paying for is the form. Use Meta's A/B test tool for 14 days at a 50/50 split, comparing the Higher Intent form with the split budget question against a More Volume form with the same question. Be honest about power. At R4,900 per arm and an illustrative R100 CPL (**ASSUMPTION**: no SA life-cover CPL was sourced), each arm yields about 49 leads. If 20% are R1,500+ (**ASSUMPTION**), that is about 10 per arm, which is too few for statistical significance. Use directional decision rules instead: keep the cheaper arm on cost per verified R1,500+ lead only if it also holds the verified-qualified rate within about 10 points, and otherwise default to Higher Intent. Apply the same logic to creative. After 14 days, cut a concept whose cost per verified qualified lead is more than 2x the best concept, and reallocate to whichever concept produces the highest R1,500+ share. Once 100+ verified R1,500+ leads accumulate, which will probably take more than one cycle, build a 1% lookalike from them as an additional hint. Watch one known risk. If Meta pushes delivery to cheap Instagram placements or to younger, renting audiences, the age-band and R1,500+ shares in the CRM will show it before CPL does.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is less about "who is the R1,500 client" and more about "what can SortMyCover actually control". The client profile is reasonably clear: a 35–50 breadwinner on R35k–R55k+ with a bond, in Gauteng's northern and eastern metros or the Western Cape. Meta in 2026 exposes almost none of those traits as targetable fields, and its policies forbid saying them back to the viewer. Affluence therefore has to be **earned through self-selection**: hard geography and an age floor to set the pond, third-person situational creative to attract the right fish, a form that tags R1,500+ without rejecting anyone, and a GoodFit signal that teaches Meta the difference. Every one of these levers is cheap to set up. None is proven for SA life cover, so cycle 1 is a measurement exercise as much as a delivery one.

Two risks deserve more attention than they usually get. If Meta forces the Financial Products Special Ad Category on SA insurance ads, the age floor and tight geography disappear in one stroke, and the plan collapses onto creative and feedback alone. That is why it is the first thing to check. Second, the evidence that direct-response life buyers skew single and non-homeowning, together with shrinking R40k–R100k payrolls, means a high-premium share is not guaranteed even with good targeting. The commercial model should therefore keep valuing R750–R1,499 leads and treat R1,500+ as upside, as the canonical decisions already do.

## Verify in Ads Manager before launch

1. Build a draft SA-only campaign with an insurance-themed ad. Check whether a Financial Products Special Ad Category prompt or restriction appears, and what it removes (age, radius floor, exclusions, lookalikes).
2. Confirm that Johannesburg, Tshwane, Midvaal, Ekurhuleni, Cape Town and Stellenbosch resolve as locations, check suburb/postcode granularity and minimum radius, and set "people living in".
3. Confirm the minimum-age control holds as a hard constraint inside Advantage+ Audience, and that custom-audience exclusions apply.
4. Search detailed targeting for small-business owners, recently moved, parents, personal finance and frequent travellers. Note which still exist for SA.
5. Confirm Audience Network can be excluded with Advantage+ placements on.
6. Check that the Higher Intent form type, multiple-choice custom questions and custom disqualification/thank-you screens are available. Read Meta's lead-ad rules on financial questions and the consent wording.
7. Check Conversion Leads eligibility, the lead-ID passback, and that the `Qualified` / `GoodFit` events arrive with the expected 1–40% conversion rate.
8. Read the full Meta Transparency Centre personal-attributes and financial-services policies. Run every ad through the breach table above. Confirm which FSP licence number appears on the ad and form (`needs_human`).
9. Do a manual Ad Library pass (SA, active, financial planning / life cover advisers, longest-running) before finalising the creative concepts.
