## 1. Compliance: does each ChatGPT angle work for an unlicensed lead-gen agency?

**The legal test**
- **VF**: FAIS defines "advice" as any "recommendation, guidance or proposal of a financial nature" about buying a financial product. It does not matter whether a transaction follows. https://www.moonstone.co.za/intermediary-service-definition-revisited/ (quoting s1), https://www.lawinsider.com/dictionary/fais-act
- **SE**: Under FAIS s1(3)(a), these are *not* advice: factual information on procedure, a product description, objective product information, "the display or distribution of promotional material", and an analysis that carries no express or implied recommendation suited to the client's needs. https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/when-does-conduct-constitute-financial-advice
- **VF**: In *Raspberry Academy v Oaksure* ([2026] ZAGPJHC 388, 14 Apr 2026), a "lead referral agreement" counted as an intermediary service, and so was unenforceable. The reason was that pay depended on policies being concluded and on premium flows. The court said labels do not decide this; the operative terms do. A flat fee per lead "may present a different factual enquiry". https://www.moonstone.co.za/referral-fees-and-fais-when-a-lead-becomes-intermediation/
- **SE**: Older industry reading: someone whose leads only *indirectly* result in a policy is not an intermediary. https://www.moonstone.co.za/leads-provision-and-the-fais-act/ (2014)
- **WH**: LV's flat-fee model is defensible on remuneration. What raises the risk is the *activity* LV appears to perform. "We'll review your cover", "compare your options" or "find out if you're overpaying" tells the consumer that someone will judge whether their product suits their needs, which is an implied recommendation. If LV's quiz or WhatsApp bot gives any verdict ("you may be underinsured", "you could save"), LV is arguably giving advice. Safe pattern: the **licensed adviser** performs the review, and the ad says so. LV only books the call.
- **VF**: Every comparison player I checked is itself an authorised FSP: Hippo (FSP 16357) https://www.hippo.co.za/, CompareGuru (FSP 47696) https://www.compareguru.co.za/life-insurance. **WH**: In SA, "compare" is a licensed activity. An unlicensed brand that markets itself as a comparison service is out of line with the market and with the regulatory default.

**Advertising rules (GCoC s14, PPR Rule 10)**
- **SE**: GCoC s14 (amended 2020) defines an advertisement as any communication meant to create public interest in a provider's financial services or products. Ads must be "factually correct, clear, accurate, balanced, and not misleading". They must not "criticise or belittle" any product or provider. Testimonials must reflect "genuine opinion and actual experience". The FSP's Key Individual must approve ads, and records are kept for 5 years. https://www.moonstone.co.za/amending-the-rules-of-advertising-how-to-keep-your-ads-safe-and-within-legal-limits/ ; the FSP licence must be disclosed on ads: https://autogen.co.za/brokers/articles/risk-mitigation/fais-general-code-of-conduct-amendments-in-perspective
- **WH**: s14 binds FSPs. LV is not one, but SMC ads promote Oracle's advice service, so Oracle carries s14 exposure for them. The practical rule: every SMC ad goes through Oracle's approval process, and the landing page names the FSP.
- **VF**: Under PPR (LTIA) Rule 10, ads by insurers must be balanced and not lead "the average targeted policyholder to any false conclusions". Comparative claims need regular independent surveys that compare equivalent features, not price alone, with the methodology made public. https://www.acts.co.za/long-term-/n1407_rule_10__advertising (this applies to insurers; **WH**: treat it as the benchmark the FSCA would apply to any "pay less" life-cover claim).
- **SE**: Direct-marketer rules require the purpose of a contact to be explained at its start, and calls to be recorded and kept for 5 years. (I verified the short-term PPR text; the long-term equivalent is similar.) https://www.acts.co.za/short-term/nppr_3_basic_rules_for_direct_marketers

**Replacement risk ("overpaying / switch")**
- **SE**: GCoC s8(1)(d) applies before advice to replace a policy. The adviser must fully disclose the costs and consequences, including waiting periods, exclusions, loadings and penalties on the new product compared with the old. https://www.moonstone.co.za/fais-ombud-highlights-fsps-obligations-when-a-policy-is-replaced/. In that case the FSP paid R800k after a replacement left a gap in cover (14 Dec 2023).
- **VF**: Under LT PPR Rule 19, the replacing insurer must get the adviser's replacement record. A senior manager must confirm it complies within 14 days, and it is sent to the replaced insurer. Undisclosed replacements must be reported to the FSCA. https://www.acts.co.za/long-term-/n1407_rule_19__replacement_of_
- **SE**: The regulator calls churning "inappropriate or unnecessary replacement of policies driven by intermediary incentives". Replacement monitoring and reporting were imposed under RDR. https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-steps-to-curb-churning/
- **WH**: "Are you paying more than you need to?" mainly attracts people who already have cover and want it cheaper. That produces replacement cases. For risk cover, replacement at age 35–50 usually means new underwriting at an older age, new waiting periods and suicide exclusions, and health changes being re-rated. Those cases are slower, more heavily documented and scrutinised for Oracle, and harder to close. This is bad lead economics as well as a compliance flag.

**Meta policy**
- **SE**: Meta's personal-attributes rule bans ads that assert or imply the viewer's age, family status, health, disability, or "vulnerable financial status". "You" is fine; implying you *know* the attribute is not. https://www.jonloomer.com/can-we-use-the-words-you-and-your-in-facebook-ads-copy/ (Meta's own policy URL returned 404 when fetched: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes)
- **WH**: Copy that would likely get flagged: "Are you underinsured?" is borderline, and "You just had a baby" is a family-status assertion. Copy that passes: "Most families have less cover than they think" (a third-person fact with a source).
- **VF**: Meta's financial-services policy requires 18+ targeting, regulatory authorisation where required, and no requests for financial info in ads. Its page does not list SA among the Special Ad Category countries. https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/financial-services/

**ASA/ARB substantiation**
- **SE**: ARB Code clause 4.1 requires documentary evidence to be held *before* publication, and survey evidence to come from a SAMRA-accredited researcher. https://www.arb.org.za/The_Codes/ (summary via search; the PDF text I fetched could not be reliably parsed. **A**: verify the exact wording.)
- **SE**: Precedent: the ARB found an OUTsurance "switch and save" testimonial misleading because it implied a like-for-like comparison. Hippo's "could have saved R300" ad drew a substantiation challenge. https://www.heraldlive.co.za/news/2019-06-12-outsurance-told-to-remove-misleading-testimonial-tv-ad/ , https://www.golegal.co.za/parody-hippo-advertisement/
- **WH**: SMC has no comparison dataset. Any "save X" or "overpaying" claim is unsubstantiated by definition, so drop it.

## 2. Product fit

- **SE**: The Santam Insurance Barometer 2025 shows that the "premium-reduction" behaviours (tracking devices, bundling motor and home, driving less, raising excesses) are **short-term insurance**. https://www.moonstone.co.za/consumers-adjusting-insurance-covers-to-cope-with-rising-cost-of-living/
- **WH**: These ChatGPT triggers are off-product for a life/risk adviser: new vehicle, driving habits, moving house (for buildings or contents), car or home renewal. They belong to Hippo, King Price and OUTsurance. Moving house only fits if it comes with a **new bond**, which is already an SMC angle.
- **SE**: SA life premiums are either age-rated (cheaper at first, rising with age; the average annual increase runs anywhere from 2% to 15%) or level/compulsory-escalation. Age-rated premiums "catch up" with level ones after about 7–10 years. https://pilotspost.com/PsPpyarn607 ; age-rated premiums can roughly "double every five years" in later years and cause shock lapses: https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-12-06-getting-to-grips-with-life-insurance-premium-patterns
- **VF**: 1Life explains increases as age-driven: years 3–5 at a fixed 5%, reviewed every 5 years with a 15% cap. https://1life.co.za/blog/premium-increase-explained. Capitec launched "no annual premium increases" life cover on 27 Jun 2024 and says premiums elsewhere typically rise 5–8% a year without more cover. https://www.moonstone.co.za/no-annual-premium-increases-on-our-new-life-product-says-capitec/
- **WH**: Annual premium escalation *is* a real review trigger for life cover. It is a recognised pain point, and Capitec built its product around it. But it pushes people toward replacing their policy. For HNW clients at R1,500+, the better outcome is usually restructuring the current policy (changing the premium pattern or reducing cover), not switching. Rephrase it as "understand why your premium went up", not "pay less".

## 3. What actually prompts SA consumers to review or buy risk cover

- **VF**: ASISA 2025 Gap Study: the gap is R50.4trn (end-2024). Cover in place replaces 39% of the income families need. The average earner needs R2.1m of life cover and has R0.8m, and needs R3m of disability cover and has R1.2m. https://www.asisa.org.za/media-releases/south-africa-s-life-and-disability-insurance-shortfall-widens-to-r504-trillion/
- **SE**: For earners above R30k a month, life-cover adequacy is about 49% and disability adequacy is "in the high thirties". Average shortfalls are above R3m (life) and R5m (disability). This comes from Old Mutual's Arno Jansen van Vuuren citing the ASISA 2025 study, in a podcast from 23 Jul 2026. https://www.cover.co.za/podcasts/why-south-africas-protection-gap-keeps-widening---a-conversation-on-underinsurance-and-the-value-of-advice-with-arno-jansen-van-vuuren
  - **WH**: The disability gap is bigger than the life gap for SMC's target group. "Your income is the asset" (the PPS framing) is under-used in SMC's angles.
- **VF**: ASISA: 8.7m risk policies lapsed in 2025 (8.2m in 2024), the first rise after three years of declines. 10.8m new policies were written, 6.2m of them funeral. https://www.asisa.org.za/media-releases/assets-managed-by-sa-life-insurers-exceed-r5-trillion-for-the-first-time/
- **VF**: Sanlam 2026 Legacy Survey (n=1,200, non-representative). Among 421 people *who have wills*, the triggers were a death in the family (41%), having a child (32%), receiving financial advice (27%), marriage (25%) and buying a home (18%). **This is about wills, not life cover.** https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61631
  - **WH**: These triggers are still useful for SMC's wills angles, and as a reasonable proxy for life events.
- **SE**: A Sanlam Risk & Savings survey found that big life events (a new child, a loss, losing a job) prompt reassessment. https://magazine.cover.co.za/june-2023-edition/are-your-clients-experiencing-reality-check-moments-midway-through-2023/. Sanlam Benchmark 2016 found price was the biggest driver in choosing a risk insurer (old). https://www.moonstone.co.za/tag/sanlam-benchmark-survey
- **SE**: Olico analysed 10,000+ applicants. The most common buyer was not married, had no kids and did not own a home (2019). https://www.moonstone.co.za/wp-content/uploads/library/Moonstone%20Library/MS%20Industry%20News/Research%20results%20reveal%20life%20insurance%20cover%20more%20popular%20thank%20you%20think.%20Olico.%205%20March%202019.%20Final%20approved_%20(3).pdf
- **Could not verify**: switching or replacement *prevalence* figures for SA risk policies (the FSCA collects replacement returns but I found no published number). I also found no NMG, Momentum/Unisa or 10X data specific to review triggers within the time available.
- **Could not verify**: any evidence that "second opinion" framing works. The search turned up nothing. **WH**: the framing still fits the evidence on low product understanding (https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/view/61), but it is untested. Treat it as a test.

## 5. WhatsApp as the primary CTA

- **SE**: WhatsApp has an 85.7% share among instant-messaging apps in SA (third-party tech tracker, methodology unclear). https://www.wmtips.com/technologies/instant-messaging/country/za/. DataReportal's Digital 2026 SA report has no WhatsApp figure. It gives Facebook 27.9m users, or 61.7% of adults. https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa
- **SE (vendor claims, low independence)**: In WhatsApp-heavy markets, click-to-WhatsApp ads are said to run 40–70% lower cost per lead than forms, with numbers verified by the platform. The same source warns: "do not assume a chat will outperform a useful landing page". Response speed and staffing decide the result. https://bitbybit.studio/tools/click-to-whatsapp-roi-calculator/ , https://www.bot.space/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-click-to-whatsapp-ads-strategy-setup-and-optimization-for-2025. I found no independent SA insurance study on lead *quality*.
- **WH**: SMC's contract qualifies leads on age 35–50, a R750+ budget and an attended booking. A quiz on a landing page captures those fields as structured data. A chat bot has to extract them through conversation, which needs a bot and someone to monitor it. Test click-to-WhatsApp as a **second** variant: same audience, same offer, measured on *attended calls per rand*, not cost per lead.
- **SE**: WhatsApp Business policy: a user messaging you opens a 24-hour service window, but "does not, in and of itself, provide… an opt-in". Business-initiated messages outside the window need prior opt-in and approved templates. Since Nov 2024, an opt-in collected on any channel counts. https://learn.rasayel.io/en/books/whatsapp/whatsapp-business/whatsapp-business-messaging-policy , https://www.cm.com/blog/whatsapp-updates-opt-in-and-quality-rating-policy/
- **Correction to the brief**: the s69 consent requirement comes from POPIA, not from Meta. Meta's opt-in rule is a separate platform policy.
- **SE**: POPIA s69 bans direct marketing by unsolicited electronic communication without consent. The Information Regulator's Dec 2024 Guidance Note counts calls and DMs as electronic communications. https://www.moonstone.co.za/spam-calls-are-now-electronic-communication-guidance-note-closes-direct-marketing-loophole/. Marketers dispute whether calls are covered, and the Regulator is seeking a court test (24 Nov 2025). https://www.moonstone.co.za/regulator-seeks-court-test-to-settle-whether-telemarketing-falls-under-popia/
- **WH**: When a user starts a chat it is solicited, so a reply is fine. Passing the number to Oracle, Oracle phoning or WhatsApping them, and any later nurture messages all need an explicit, named consent captured in the chat: "I agree that SortMyCover may share my details with [adviser/FSP] to contact me by phone/WhatsApp". It must be logged with a timestamp, the same as on the form.

## 6. Higgsfield and AI-generated people

- **VF**: Higgsfield is an AI image and video generation platform that aggregates models (Sora 2, Veo 3.1, Kling, Seedance, Nano Banana and others). An **official hosted MCP** is live at `https://mcp.higgsfield.ai/mcp`. It uses OAuth with no API key, needs a paid plan, and *every* MCP generation uses credits (website "unlimited" plans do not apply). There is also a separate REST API at api.higgsfield.ai, plus a CLI. https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/integrations/what-is-higgsfield-mcp. Launch date reported as 30 Apr 2026 (secondary: https://mcp.directory/blog/higgsfield-mcp-guide).
- **VF**: Meta: when an ad contains an AI-generated photorealistic human, the "AI info" label appears **next to "Sponsored"**. Meta detects third-party AI tools through industry signals (update dated 1 Jun 2026). https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/. Self-disclosure is mandatory only for political and social-issue ads (same source plus https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-outlines-new-rules-on-the-use-of-ai-generated-content-in-political-ads/699227/).
  - **SE (secondary, unverified)**: one source says undisclosed AI content is now a reason for ad rejection. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-ai-ad-labels-mandatory-disclosure-ecommerce-2026
- **SE**: The FSCA names deepfake and AI-impersonation financial ads as a top enforcement priority for 2025/26. https://www.itweb.co.za/article/fsca-targets-deepfakes-in-financial-scams-crackdown/lwrKx73Y1xaqmg1o
- **WH**:
  - Do not show AI people as clients, testimonials or advisers. GCoC s14 requires endorsements to reflect "actual experience", the ARB has precedent on misleading testimonials, and an "AI info" label next to a financial ad reads like a scam signal in the current FSCA climate.
  - AI is acceptable for b-roll, objects, abstract or illustrative scenes, and clearly stylised characters.
  - Use real footage of the real adviser (Mark).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overpayment ("paying more than you need to?") | Partly (life premiums do escalate) | **No**: unsubstantiated savings claim (ARB 4.1), invites replacement (s8(1)(d), Rule 19), comparison is FSP territory, Hippo owns it | "Know why your premium goes up each year. Ask a licensed adviser." | Strong (against) | **Kill** (keep the rephrase as Test) |
| Underinsurance ("would it actually protect you?") | Yes | Borderline: "you" plus an implied gap. | Forgotten policy ("when last did you review?") | Yes | OK, provided the adviser does the review, not SMC | "Cover taken out at 30 rarely fits at 45. A licensed adviser can walk you through yours." | Medium (lapse data, life-event research) | **Tier 2** |
| Life changed, policy didn't (marriage, children, income) | Yes | OK. Avoid asserting family status ("You just…") | "Marriage, kids, a bigger salary: each changes what cover should do." | Medium-strong (Sanlam triggers, BrightRock owns "needs-matched") | **Tier 1** (as a frame over existing bond/baby/salary pages) |

**Bottom line (WH)**:
- Keep SMC as a life-event and gap brand that books a named, licensed, broker-neutral adviser.
- Leave "save, compare, overpaying" to the FSP aggregators (Hippo). For a flat-fee unlicensed lead generator, those messages carry the most regulatory risk, and they pull in replacement-heavy, price-driven leads, which is the opposite of an HNW R1,500+ profile.
- New Tier-1 additions: sourced underinsurance (life **and disability**) for R30k+ earners, and "life changed, policy didn't" as an umbrella over the existing life-event pages.
