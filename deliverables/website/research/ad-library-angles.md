# Meta Ad Library field research: life cover, long-term insurance and wills/estate (South Africa)

Collected 10 Oct 2026 from the public Meta Ad Library (country South Africa, All ads, Active ads, sorted by impressions where the filter allowed). No login, no ad clicks, no forms. Run in my own Chrome tab (closed at the end); the Hostinger and Search Console tabs were not touched.

Files: `ad-library/ad-catalog.csv` (every record), `ad-library/*.jpg` (16 evidence screenshots, named advertiser-angle-started-date).

## Read this first: what the numbers are and are not

- **Longevity is a proxy, not proof.** "Started running on" is the first date of that specific ad. A 130-day ad may be a brand ad on a huge budget, an always-on adviser page nobody is optimising, or a re-launched creative. Meta shows no spend, no results, no CTR for commercial ads in South Africa. Treat "60+ days" and "many variants" as "probably not losing money", nothing stronger.
- **Counts are samples.** Each search renders about 20-30 ads before I stopped scrolling. The catalog has **132 creative-group rows from 74 advertisers** (about 240-300 individual active ad records once you count duplicates; Dis-Chem alone has 14+ in one feed and Momentum has 22 copies of one creative). 111 rows / 59 advertisers are relevant (non-funeral, non-short-term, not CPD training); 71 rows are long-term insurance (theme A), 40 are wills/estate (theme B). Funeral, short-term, medical and CPD-training advertisers were captured only to understand the market and are excluded from the rankings.
- Ads are grouped by me, by hand-written rules, into angles; some ads fit two angles and were put in the closest one.
- Platforms, versions and creative text inside images are only visible in screenshots (16 captured). The CSV does not carry platform; from the screenshots almost every ad runs on Facebook + Instagram, many also Messenger, and some Threads/Audience Network. Video ads show "multiple versions" more often than static.
- Excluded as noise: a large cluster of fake-health "story" ads (shop.pipitea.com and similar) and short-drama app ads that match "life cover"/"will" in body text; "sarb.path-2-career.com" clickbait that copies a wills page.

### Who is advertising wills: attorneys/fiduciary vs FAIS-authorised planners

Inferred from the page name and ad copy only. I did **not** check the FSCA register, the Legal Practice Council or CIPC, so treat these labels as unverified.

- **Attorneys / law firms:** Keshrina Mahabeer Attorneys, Conradie Inc. Attorneys, Woodhead Bigby Attorneys, Mc Naught Company Attorneys, Johann Viljoen Associates Attorneys. They sell the legal instrument itself, use Messenger/WhatsApp CTAs, and can say "valid will", "Intestate Succession Act", "executor" in their own right.
- **Bank fiduciary arms / will-drafting providers:** Nedbank (will-drafting page, "speak to a financial adviser"), FNB Fiduciary, Capital Legacy (appears to be a dedicated will/fiduciary provider; status unverified), Trustable (online will), Proud Mom (says it matches people to "registered legal service providers"; its own status unverified).
- **Insurers / FAIS-style product houses using will offers to feed advice:** Discovery (+ adviser-influencer Didi Khumalo), PPS, Sanlam, Old Mutual (will helpline), Momentum, MiWayLife, Clientele (legal cover product mentions "drawing up a Will").
- **Independent / tied planners:** Sanlam BlueStar network pages mention wills as one line in "savings, insurance or wills"; Atfin Consulting, Prestige Wealth, Baroka, Your Legacy, Geoff Dore. None runs a wills-first campaign.

Implication for us: an adviser ad that promises "a valid will" or "legal advice" strays into attorney territory. The insurers and PPS keep to "start the estate-planning conversation" and link out to a will provider. That is the safe wording model for FAIS advisers.

## Hook patterns that repeat

1. **Question to the reader:** "What happens to your bond if it doesn't go as planned?" (Black Cygnet), "If something happens to me, will my children be okay?" (independent adviser creative), "Is your protection still right for you?", "Still procrastinating over your will?", "Thinking about life insurance?".
2. **Reality-of-life then reassurance:** "Life is unpredictable. ..." (Momentum, Old Mutual), "Accidents happen when you least expect it" (Liberty), "It's crazy how quickly life can change" (Liberty).
3. **Qualifier call-out:** "If you own a business and employ people, this one's for you" (Simply), "If you have R5 million+ ... within 5-10 years of retirement" (planner), "British Expats ... R10 million or more", "Declined Life Cover due to your health?" (AllLife).
4. **Short imperative trio:** "Make your wishes known. Put them in writing. Draft your Will today." (Discovery).
5. **Statistic / authority line:** "Over 50% of South Africans don't have a valid will", "paid out R924 million in claims", "Assupol protecting families for 110 years".
6. **Myth-bust:** "'Wills are expensive.' That's a myth."
7. **Humour or a recurring character:** Old Mutual's "Dad-viser", Fit Wealth Club "That's skipping leg day", Metropolitan's Gogo Vivian (funeral).
8. **Persona in the hook (price anchor):** "Female 30, non-smoker", "50-year-old male, non-smoker".
9. **Identity / hobby:** "You train, plan and prepare for every ride" (cycling), "It takes courage to start a side hustle", "When you are young, driven... building an empire".

## Formats that repeat

- Of the 111 relevant rows: **63 static**, **38 video**, **10 carousel/mixed**. Video is rare for wills (attorneys use a single static graphic repeated across 4-5 ads), common for Liberty, Tigris, Sanlam, Nedbank, Absa.
- **No UGC, no real customer testimonial, no talking-head adviser videos for life cover** in the sample except Tigris (founder-style talking head, vertical video) and Didi Khumalo (influencer-adviser, vertical). Testimonials that exist are prize-winner stories (1Life, Clientele) not policyholder stories.
- **One creative copied across 4-22 ad records** is the norm (Dis-Chem, Momentum, Liberty, AllLife): advertisers run the same asset in many ad sets and change audience, not the creative.
- Creative images carry the specifics (price, cover amount, "99.8% payout rate"), not the primary text. The DOM text alone understates how price-led the market is.
- CTA buttons: "Learn more" dominates (44 rows incl. spelling variants), "Get quote" 24, "Send message" 5, "Apply now" 5, "Book now" 2. Wills advertisers use "Learn more"/"Apply now"; independents use "Send message" (Messenger/Instagram DM); only a few use WhatsApp (PreNorth BlueStar, an attorney, funeral micro-insurance).
- Destinations: website quote page (insurers), native lead form (Liberty, Tigris, Atfin, Marco du Toit), Messenger/DM (independents, attorneys), calculator (Different Life), will-qualify checker (Proud Mom). No quiz funnel seen.

## Compliance patterns: what the market does that we may not copy

Seen in the sample (57 of 132 catalog rows flagged); use as a "do not copy" list:

- **Named products or programmes:** Wealth Bonus, Instant Life Cover, Flexible Life Plan, 4/4 Strategy, FNB Life Legacy Plan, Dis-Chem Chronic Life Cover.
- **Premiums and cover amounts:** "from R99 per month", "R1 million life cover from R136 p/m", "R2 million from R200 pm", "R5.07 million life cover", "from R75/month, no medicals", "R300/month RA", an independent adviser's creative showing "R1.2m / R1.5m / R214".
- **Comparison and price superlatives:** "Tired of expensive life cover", "Stop overpaying", "up to 35% off", "save up to 50% on premiums", "affordable". (I saw no literal "cheapest" in life ads, but "affordable" and "expensive" are everywhere.)
- **Guarantees and speed promises:** "activate your cover in 30 minutes", "Instant Life Cover", "pays out within 24 hours", "99.8% payout rate", "No medical", "no paperwork", "up to 100% of net income replaced".
- **Tax and return claims:** "tax-free lump sum", "up to 50% comes back to you at 65, tax-free", "SARS can take up to 25%", "every rand... reduces your tax bill by 36 cents".
- **Statistics as proof:** "R924 million paid in 2025", "R1.2 billion", "Over 50% of South Africans don't have a valid will" (source rarely shown).
- **Prize competitions and free-offer lures:** 1Life R5 000 weekly, Clientele Spin-and-Win, free wills.
- **Client stories:** an independent adviser used an anonymised client story ("Sarah (name changed) is 31") on Messenger; consent and FAIS disclosure risk.
- **Foreign/unlicensed look-alikes:** Oracle Life (Eswatini) ads run in South Africa; do not treat as a model.

Clean patterns worth copying structurally: question hooks, scenario-list hooks (Black Cygnet), qualifier call-outs, adviser-conversation framing (Old Mutual), seasonal timing (Wills Week), will-to-cover bridge (MiWayLife, FNB executor-fees logic), "no sales pitch, number first" ordering (Tigris) without the number.

## Gaps and white space (age 35-50, R1,500+/month budgets, owners/directors/parents/bond holders)

Checked in the library; low or no presence:

1. **Business owners and directors, long-term cover:** "key person" returned **no relevant ads**; "buy and sell" not run (but business insurance results were all short-term: Uber drivers, boutiques, SMEs). Only Simply staff cover and Tigris's 4/4 risk-list touch it. Nobody is selling key-person, shareholder buy-and-sell, or director loan-account cover, and Tigris is the only one speaking to "debt, partners, key people, payroll".
2. **Self-employed / freelancers:** searching "life cover self-employed" returned only an expat insurer (APRIL International). No SA-native self-employed income-protection angle.
3. **Age 35-50 as an explicit audience:** there is no "turning 40" or "in your 40s" ad. Personas appear only as price anchors (30 f, 50 m). The "peak responsibility years" (bond + kids + parents) are unspoken.
4. **R1,500+/month budgets:** every price-led ad targets R99-R500 cover or R75 bond cover. Investec is the only premium voice, and its creative is brand-level. A "serious cover for people with real obligations" voice is open.
5. **Bond holders:** Black Cygnet is the single advertiser (bond protection), only since 4 Sep, using scenario-list hooks. No life-event "just signed the bond" ad from an adviser.
6. **Parents:** parent/guardian is strong in wills, nearly absent in life cover (just generic "your family's future"). "Who raises them, who pays the bond, who pays school fees" is a bridge that nobody ties together.
7. **Will to cover to liquidity as one story:** only FNB (executor fees) and MiWayLife (will is not enough) connect these, each in one or two ads. A will-first funnel that ends in cover review is nearly unclaimed for independent advisers.
8. **Testimonial / proof by real clients:** none for life cover (regulatory care required, but the lane is empty). Proof is brand-level claims (R924m) or prizes.
9. **WhatsApp / "free call" as a conversion path for life cover:** WhatsApp appears for funeral micro-insurance, medical and a couple of planners, not for long-term cover. No "book a free call" angle for life cover beyond Liberty's "Book Now".
10. **Wills week timing:** a seasonal burst runs 30 Sep-9 Oct. A second wave (Nov-Jan, "before the holidays", "new year admin", "after a bond is signed") is not currently contested.
11. **Quiz / needs-gap tools:** only Different Life has a calculator, and it is a price quote in disguise.

## What I did not verify or could not do

- **Spend, impressions, CTR, conversions:** not available. Everything about "working" is a proxy.
- **Why a given ad is long-running** (a brand with a huge budget vs a small tested ad): unknown. Dis-Chem, Momentum, Sanlam, Old Mutual, Discovery likely run always-on brand budgets.
- **Complete coverage:** each query rendered about 20-30 ads. Not run: "free call" and "get a quote" (generic), "buy and sell", "directors", "inheritance" (partly covered by "executor"/"estate duty"), "trust", "turned 40", and advertiser-specific searches for Outsurance, Hippo, Dial Direct, Budget Insurance, Altron FinTech (short-term insurers, expected out of scope). 1Life, Sanlam, Old Mutual, Momentum, Liberty, Discovery, Hollard, Clientele, PPS, Assupol, Brightrock, Investec, Absa, Capitec, Standard Bank, FNB, Nedbank and Dis-Chem were seen through keyword or name searches, not by exhaustive per-advertiser crawl.
- **Platform per ad** is not in the CSV (only visible in screenshots). Version counts are as shown on the card ("N ads use this creative and text") and only appear when Meta groups them.
- **FAIS / LPC status** of any advertiser: not checked.
- **Landing pages:** not opened (no clicking ads). Destination is inferred from the card's displayed domain/CTA.
- **Library IDs for two will ads** (Proud Mom Oct 2026 and Keep Save) were transcribed by hand from extraction output, so double-check before linking.
- **Date handling:** run date taken as 10 Oct 2026; "days running" = 10 Oct minus start date.
- **Environment note:** I briefly started a local helper script to receive scraped data, the permission layer blocked the browser from posting to it, so I deleted it and used on-page extraction instead. While stopping it I ran a blanket process kill on python.exe, which may have ended another Python process on this machine; nothing in this project depended on it, but worth knowing if something Python-based stopped around that time.
