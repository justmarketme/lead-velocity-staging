## 1. The judgment - *The Raspberry Academy (Pty) Ltd v Oaksure Financial Services (Pty) Ltd* (Grade A)

**Citation:** (2025/219635) [2026] ZAGPJHC 388 (14 April 2026), Gauteng Division, Johannesburg, DJ Smit AJ. Marked "Reportable: No". Also cited as 2026 JDR 1647 (GJ) (FAnews/Eversheds). Deneys cites it as [2026] ZAGPJHC **263** on LawLibrary - the SAFLII number is 388; use 388 and check the LawLibrary number before filing anything.
**Link (read in full):** https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAGPJHC/2026/388.html

**Facts.** Default-judgment application on a 2019 "Lead Referral Agreement"; claim R1,214,698.62 arrear "commissions" plus accounting. Oaksure did not defend. The court raised legality of its own accord and asked for heads [3]-[4]. Raspberry had been paid almost R6 million over the agreement's life [6].

**What the agreement said (the operative terms the court relied on):**
- Clause 7 fee: a percentage of "Net Premium" on active, paid-up "Net Policies"; due only once Oaksure received the policyholder's payment; payable for as long as the policies stayed in force, even after termination [9]. Annexure C: 10% of net premium (passenger liability) and 50% of statutory commission on other lines [10].
- Clause 4: Oaksure appointed Raspberry "to market" its short-term products to the leads [28b].
- Definition of "Referral": any action that "directly results" in a person contacting Oaksure for a quotation or sale "with a view to concluding a Policy" [28a].
- Clause 6: Raspberry would generate leads "for the benefit of Oaksure", arrange consent to pass the information on, and tell the person Oaksure would contact them to "discuss appropriate cover" [28c]; Oaksure would give marketing assistance [28d].
- Control and feedback: Raspberry could ask in writing that a specific product not be offered to a lead and Oaksure would comply [29a]; Oaksure had to notify Raspberry of issues arising from referrals [29b].
- Clause 9.5: Raspberry would not act as an insurance intermediary; it would act "as marketing agent and lead referrer only" [12].

**What the court held and why.**
- Question framed under FAIS s1 "intermediary service" para (a): an act, other than advice, "for or on behalf of a client or product supplier", the result of which is that a client "may enter into, offers to enter into or enters into" a transaction with a product supplier [17]. The defining feature is a causal link between the act and the (potential) transaction [19], read with *Tristar* (intermediary = a "go-between" interposed between client and product supplier) [18].
- "The clearest indication" that the services were intermediary services: Raspberry was paid **only where a lead actually entered into** a policy transaction [25].
- If Raspberry had merely supplied contact details, there "could have been a factual debate" about how the details were obtained, in what context, "with what disclosure to the client and for what purpose"; merely passing on contact details "does not necessarily" reach the point where a lead "may enter" a transaction [26].
- The "most telling feature" was remuneration: not "a flat fee for each Lead referred, which may have suggested a mechanical recording of details and passing them on", but a percentage of successful referrals for the life of the policy [27].
- Further indicators: the causal "Referral" definition, the appointment "to market" the products, the consent and "discuss appropriate cover" obligations, the marketing assistance [28]; and Raspberry's control over which products were offered plus the issue-reporting duty showed the activity was not the mechanical kind excluded from "representative" [29].
- The label in clause 9.5 failed because the remuneration showed Raspberry was paid "not for referring leads, but for effecting introductions and marketing that directly result in transactions" [30].
- Raspberry was neither an authorised FSP nor a representative, so it was acting illegally under FAIS s7 [31]; agreements contravening intermediary-services legislation are unenforceable for illegality [32] (citing *Absa Insurance Brokers v Luttig* 1997 (4) SA 229 (SCA), *Afrisure CC v Watson NO* 2009 (2) SA 127 (SCA), *KGA Life v Multisure* 2024 (3) SA 51 (SCA) - cited in fn 11, not separately read). The court also noted the Short-term Insurance Act intermediary definition (stricter causation) [21]-[23].
- Order: default judgment dismissed, no order as to costs [35].

**What a compliant lead generator would look like (court's own signals, not a safe harbour):** paid a flat fee per lead, not contingent on a policy [27]; activity limited to obtaining and passing on contact details with proper disclosure and purpose [26]; no appointment "to market" a product; no "discuss appropriate cover" script; no say over which products the FSP offers; no causal-to-sale definitions [28]-[29]. The court expressly did **not** decide that flat-fee lead generation is outside FAIS - it said there "could have been a factual debate" [26].

**How it shaped the draft.**
- Fee clause: flat cycle fee and flat per-lead top-up (R16,500 / 20 leads; R850 top-up), payable in advance, never contingent on, calculated by reference to, or varied by any application, policy, premium or commission; no trailing or post-termination entitlement of any kind (the opposite of Raspberry clause 7). [LAWYER REVIEW]
- Definitions: "Qualified Lead" and "Introduction" are defined by the consumer's own acts (response, consent, self-declared criteria, booking) - never "with a view to concluding a policy", never "results in a sale". No word "referral fee", "commission" or "success".
- Services clause: Lead Velocity markets **its own lead-generation service and the SortMyCover brand**, never "the Client's products"; it is not appointed to market any financial product.
- Consumer messaging: Lead Velocity tells the consumer only that an authorised financial adviser (named, with FSP number) will meet them for an introductory call; never "to discuss appropriate cover" or similar. Product/cover/premium questions are deferred to the Client with a fixed line.
- No control: Lead Velocity has no right to influence which products, insurers or premiums the Client offers; the Client has no duty to report sale outcomes to Lead Velocity.
- Substance-over-form covenant: both parties must operate the relationship as written, and any practice that departs from it is a breach. [LAWYER REVIEW]

## 2. FAIS Act 37 of 2002 - s1 definitions, s1(3) exclusions, s7 (Grade A)

**Links:** SAFLII consolidated Act https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/faaisa2002423/ ; LawLibrary https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/2002/37/eng@2002-11-15 ; Acts Online s1 https://www.acts.co.za/financial-advisory-and-intermediary-services-act-2002/1_definitions_and_application

**Summary.**
- "Advice" = any recommendation, guidance or proposal of a financial nature to a client about purchasing, investing in, transacting on, varying, replacing or terminating a financial product. **s1(3)(a) excludes** factual advice given merely on the procedure for entering into a transaction, describing a product, answering routine administrative queries, objective product information, or "display or distribution of promotional material"; and an analysis/report without any express or implied recommendation that a transaction suits the client's objectives, situation or needs.
- "Intermediary service" = any act other than advice, performed "for or on behalf of a client or product supplier", (a) the result of which is that a client may enter into, offers to enter into or enters into a transaction with a product supplier; or (b) with a view to dealing in/administering/servicing a product, collecting premiums, or handling claims. **s1(3)(b) exclusions** are narrow (bank conduit for premiums, product suppliers regulated under their own law, registrar exemptions) - none applies to a lead generator.
- "Client" = a specific person or group, "excluding the general public". "Product supplier" = the issuer of the financial product (here, the life insurer - not the broker).
- **Correction to the brief:** the "does not require judgment" / "does not lead a client to any specific transaction in response to general enquiries" wording is **not** an exclusion from "advice" or "intermediary service". It is an exclusion from the definition of **"representative"** (persons rendering clerical, technical, administrative or other services in a subsidiary or subordinate capacity). The judgment applies it in that way [15], [29].
- s7(1): no one may act as an FSP without a licence, or as a representative unless appointed under s13. s7(2): a policy concluded through an unauthorised person is not unenforceable between insurer and client merely for that reason. **s7(3): an authorised FSP may only conduct financial-services-related business with a person rendering financial services if that person is licensed where required** - this is the Client's own exposure if Lead Velocity is ever characterised as an intermediary.

**How it shaped the draft.**
- Prohibited-acts list mirrors the statutory language: no recommendation, guidance or proposal; no comparison, quote or premium discussion; no act whose result is that a consumer enters or offers to enter into a transaction; no application, premium or claim handling (para (b)).
- Permitted acts are described in the language of the s1(3)(a) exclusions and the "representative" carve-out: display/distribution of general promotional material, logistics, routine administrative answers (time, link, reschedule), all without judgment about any product or the consumer's needs.
- Pre-qualification is limited to self-declared, objective criteria (age band, self-stated monthly budget band). Lead Velocity does not assess suitability, need, health or affordability, and does not tell a consumer they do or do not "qualify" for cover - only whether they fit the campaign's introduction criteria.
- Booking clause: the consumer picks a slot from the Client's published availability; Lead Velocity exercises no judgment and makes no representation about outcomes.
- Mutual warranty plus Client acknowledgement of s7(3): the Client confirms it has assessed the model and that Lead Velocity renders no financial service. [LAWYER REVIEW]
- Lead Velocity acts for its own account (independent contractor), not "for or on behalf of a client or product supplier"; it has no relationship with any insurer.

## 3. *Tristar Investments (Pty) Ltd v Chemical Industries National Provident Fund* [2013] ZASCA 59 (Grade A - as cited in Raspberry [18]; not separately re-read)

**Link:** cited at Raspberry fn 8; summarised by CDH and ENS (below).
**Summary.** SCA: an intermediary is a genuine "go-between" interposed between a client and a product supplier; para (a) of the definition contemplates acts that **directly** result in the transaction; the definition must not be read to catch remote or indirect activity (as reported by ENS/CDH).
**How it shaped the draft.** Supports keeping Lead Velocity outside the client-insurer axis: it never deals with any insurer, never stands between the consumer and the product supplier, and its last act is a booked introductory meeting with the Client (who then advises). The agreement records that the Client, not Lead Velocity, is the only party who deals with any product supplier.

## 4. FSB/FSCA guidance on referrals, lead generation and introducers

### 4.1 FSB Retail Distribution Review (RDR), November 2014 (Grade A - regulator policy document, not law)
**Link (mirror read in full):** https://www.masthead.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/FSB-Retail-Distribution-Review-2014.pdf (official FSCA copy: UNVERIFIED link).
**Summary.** Para 2.2.9 describes a "lead provider" as one who gives an intermediary or product supplier names/contact details of potential customers, or gives a customer the details of an intermediary, typically remunerated "in some form"; states the FAIS framework "does not regulate the provision of referrals and leads or their remuneration" unless the conflict-of-interest provisions apply, and that remuneration might fall under insurance commission regulations where the lead relates to entering into a policy. Proposal I: "certain specifically identified forms" of referrals will be treated as intermediation with conduct standards on remuneration disclosure, conflicts, consent and data protection; others may fall under marketing/advertising or outsourcing standards. Proposal XX: review of remuneration for referrals/leads. The FSCA's 2018 RDR status update lists Proposals I and XX as still to be developed (Clyde & Co copy of the FSCA update: https://www.clydeco.com/clyde/media/fileslibrary/FSCARetailDistributionReviewStatusUpdateJune2018....pdf). We found **no final FSCA conduct standard on lead generation or referral fees** as at 5 Oct 2026 (absence not proven - flag for the attorney).
**How it shaped the draft.** The agreement records the activity as lead provision (contact details + introduction), with remuneration disclosed, fixed and unconnected to products; consent and data-protection mechanics are built in (as RDR Proposal I anticipates). A change-in-law clause lets either party amend or exit if the FSCA issues a lead-generation or referral-fee standard. [LAWYER REVIEW]

### 4.2 FAIS General Code of Conduct (BN 80 of 2003 as amended by BN 706 of 2020) - s3A and s14 (Grade A)
**Link (consolidated copy read):** https://www.masthead.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/BN-80-of-2003-FAIS-GCOC-June-2020.pdf
**Summary.** s3A(1)(a) lists the only financial interests a provider may receive from or offer to a "third party", including (v) "fees or remuneration for the rendering of a service to a third party" and (vii) a financial interest for which reasonably commensurate value is paid. s14 (advertising): "advertisement" = communication intended to create public interest in a provider's business or services; s14(1)(a) "client" includes the general public; s14(2)(d) where another person produces an advertisement, the provider must ensure consistency if that person acts on its behalf, or take reasonable mitigation steps if it merely knows of it; s14(3) ads must be factually correct, balanced and not misleading, with specific rules for references to premiums.
**How it shaped the draft.** Fee is a commensurate charge for a defined marketing service (s3A(1)(a)(v)/(vii) logic), not a share of any product remuneration. Advertising clause: SortMyCover creative stays broker-neutral and product-free (no insurer names, premiums, cover amounts or comparisons); the Client gets view access and a right to require withdrawal of anything inconsistent with s14, without making Lead Velocity its agent. [LAWYER REVIEW]

### 4.3 FSB Guidance Note on intermediary services and representatives (Grade A content via mirror; official reference UNVERIFIED)
**Link (mirror):** https://skillfully.co.za/legislation/fais-guidance-note-on-intermediary-services-and-representatives/
**Summary.** The "representative" exclusions limit who must be a representative, but do **not** mean no intermediary service is rendered: where a person performs a mechanical act for an FSP, the FSP itself renders the intermediary service. The registrar looks at surrounding circumstances; a provider claiming its people "do not exercise any judgment" must be able to substantiate it. Explaining policy features requires judgment.
**How it shaped the draft.** Records and evidence clause: Lead Velocity keeps scripts, flows and message logs showing no judgment is exercised and no product is explained, and makes them available to the Client and any regulator. Also an alternative structure for the attorney (Risk note R2).

### 4.4 FSCA Regulatory Actions Report 2025/26 - "referral" enforcement (UNVERIFIED primary; reported by Moneyweb, 3 Aug 2026)
**Link:** https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/south-africa/fsca-targets-unlawful-referrals-and-client-data-abuse/
**Summary.** FSCA flagged businesses calling themselves referral businesses while rendering unlicensed financial services; problems arise where a referrer explains products, assists with applications or is involved in the investment process. Case study: R1 million penalty on a company and 10-year debarment of a representative, upheld by the Financial Services Tribunal. FSCA also flagged misuse of client personal information.
**How it shaped the draft.** Reinforces the hard prohibitions (no product explanation, no application assistance) and the Client's covenant not to ask Lead Velocity to do any of these.

## 8. ECT Act 25 of 2002 - s12, s13, s22 (Grade A)

**Links:** s13 https://www.acts.co.za/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act/13__signature ; *Spring Forest Trading 599 CC v Wilberry (Pty) Ltd* (725/13) [2014] ZASCA 178 https://www.supremecourtofappeal.org.za/index.php/component/jdownloads/send/29-judgments-2014/2641-spring-forest-trading-v-wilberry-725-13-2014-zasca-178-21-november-2014?Itemid=0
**Summary.**
- s12: a legal requirement for writing is met by a data message (subject to excluded categories such as alienation of land, long leases, wills, bills of exchange - none relevant here).
- s13(1): where **the law** requires a signature and does not specify its type, only an **advanced electronic signature** (accredited process) suffices. s13(2): an electronic signature is not without force merely because it is electronic. s13(3): where **the parties** require a signature without agreeing on a type, any method that identifies the person and indicates approval, reliable as appropriate, suffices. s13(4): AES presumed valid.
- s22(1): an agreement is not without legal force merely because concluded by data messages.
- *Spring Forest*: s13(1) applies only to signatures required by law, not to party-imposed formalities (e.g. a non-variation clause); typed names at the foot of emails were valid signatures under s13(3) [18]-[28].
**Is an AES needed for this agreement?** No law we found requires a services agreement of this kind to be signed, so s13(1) is not triggered and an ordinary electronic signature (platform click-sign, typed name with audit trail) is valid. The only signature requirement is the one the parties create themselves. One point to check: POPIA Form 4 contains a "signature of data subject" line - the Regulator's guidance accepts an electronic "I give my consent" (GN 7.2.5), so we treat consumer consent as not needing an AES (Risk note R11).
**How it shaped the draft.** Signature clause names the agreed type of e-signature (s13(3)): [named e-sign platform or portal acceptance with name, timestamp, IP and document hash], plus counterparts. Variation clause: because *Spring Forest* means emails with typed names can satisfy "signed in writing", the draft says variations bind only if made through [the signing platform / a document expressly titled "Variation" and e-signed by authorised signatories] - stops casual emails varying fees or the Qualified Lead definition. Notices by email valid (s12, s22).

## Risk notes for the drafter

**R1 - Brief misplaces the FAIS exclusion (correct before drafting).** The "no judgment / general enquiries" carve-out is in the definition of "representative", not "advice" or "intermediary service" (FAIS s1; Raspberry [15], [29]). It cannot be pleaded as "our acts are excluded from intermediary services". The real arguments are: (a) Lead Velocity's acts lack the causal link to a transaction with a product supplier (Raspberry [19], [26]; *Tristar*); (b) it acts for its own account, not "for or on behalf of a client or product supplier"; (c) any advice-like statements fall within s1(3)(a) factual/promotional exclusions. Draft the recitals on those three bases.

**R2 - Alternative structure for the attorney to weigh.** Per the FSB Guidance Note (4.3), a mechanical, no-judgment service performed *for an FSP* can mean the FSP itself is rendering the intermediary service and the helper is not a representative. That is a different, arguably stronger, defence than "independent marketing agency", but it conflicts with the brief's "no agency / not on behalf of" framing. Pick one; do not mix them.

**R3 - Qualified Lead = booked AND attendance confirmed (moderate).** This is not policy-linked, so it is on the safe side of Raspberry [25]/[27]. But it moves the billable unit one step further down the sales funnel than "contact details passed on" - the only example the court described as possibly mechanical [26]-[27]. An opposing party could argue Lead Velocity is paid for producing a meeting with an adviser whose purpose is a sale (ENS: "functional part of the sales process"). Mitigations in draft: unit defined by the consumer's own booking and confirmation, not by meeting outcome; fee earned at confirmation regardless of attendance; no-show replacement is discretionary goodwill, never for "didn't buy"; no outcome data used in any fee, credit or replacement. Practitioner should confirm. [LAWYER REVIEW]

**R4 - Per-lead top-up pricing (low).** R850 per Qualified Lead is a flat fee per lead - exactly what the court said "may have suggested" mechanical activity [27]. Volume tiers (Silver/Gold) are fine if priced on leads, never on policies, premiums or conversion. Risk rises only if any tier, discount or bonus references sales or the Client's revenue.

**R5 - Lookalike audiences and retargeting need separate, specific consent (moderate-high).** POPIA s13/s18 require a specific, notified purpose; the Regulator's guidance lists **cookies** as an electronic direct-marketing method (GN 7.1(h)) and treats lead generation/profiling as needing full compliance (10.4). Uploading hashed lead data to Meta is a disclosure to an offshore party (s72) and further processing (s15). Bundling "marketing optimisation including lookalike audiences and retargeting" into the single introduction consent is weak: it is not necessary for the introduction and may fail the "voluntary, specific" consent standard. Recommend a separate, optional, unticked consent (or at minimum a separate sentence with an objection route, plus a documented legitimate-interest assessment), and retargeting of consumers who have not consented only on a lawful basis the attorney confirms. Meta acting as processor for Custom Audiences is our understanding of Meta's terms - UNVERIFIED. Prior project Q8/Q9 already pose this question.

**R6 - Messaging must not "warm" the lead (moderate).** Raspberry clause 6 required telling leads the insurer would "discuss appropriate cover" [28c]; Deneys/McCarthy treat "warming a lead" as intermediation. Nurture reminders up to 10 minutes before the call, and the first WhatsApp disclosure, must be logistics-only (time, link, adviser name, practice, FSP number, reschedule, STOP). No benefit statements, no "find the right cover", no premium ranges. The R750-R1,250 budget band should be asked as a neutral self-declaration and never echoed back as a price.

**R7 - "Neither is the other's operator" may not hold for reminders and calendar work (moderate).** After delivery, Lead Velocity still sends reminders for the Client's appointment and books into the Client's calendar. That is arguably processing for the Client's purpose (operator, s20-21). Options: (a) keep reminders as Lead Velocity's own purpose, disclosed in its notice (our default), or (b) add a short s21 operator schedule limited to calendar and reminder processing. The prior agreement drafts used (b) (practitioner-brief Q7). [LAWYER REVIEW]

**R8 - Feedback and outcome data (moderate).** Raspberry [29b] treated the insurer's duty to report issues to the referrer as a sign of non-mechanical involvement. The brief makes the Client give "feedback on leads"; prior design collects a voluntary "policies written" figure (practitioner-brief Q3). Limit mandatory feedback to attendance and contactability. If any outcome data is collected, it must be voluntary, never used in fees, credits, replacements or pricing, and firewalled in the agreement.

**R9 - "No refunds" and CPA (moderate - depends on Client's legal form).** The brief names the Client as "[MARK - FULL NAME], trading as [PRACTICE NAME]". A natural person trading under a name is **not** a juristic person, so the s5(2)(b) R2m exemption does not apply and the CPA can apply in full. If the contracting party is a company/CC/trust with assets or turnover of R2m or more, the CPA is excluded. Where the CPA applies: s48-s51 (unfair terms), s49 (conspicuous notice of liability cap, sole remedy, no refund), s54 (refund remedy for services not performed to standard - may collide with "rollover is the sole remedy"), and s17 (cancellation of an advance order, subject to a reasonable charge). Draft: a Client-status declaration, a CPA saving clause, and fallback wording (a reasonable charge for committed media spend, pro-rata credit after rollover) that applies when the CPA applies. Recommend contracting with the Client's juristic entity where one exists. [LAWYER REVIEW]

**R10 - Illegality cuts both ways (moderate, for the attorney).** If the agreement were ever held to be an unlicensed intermediary arrangement, Lead Velocity could not enforce payment (Raspberry [32]) and the Client would itself be exposed under FAIS s7(3). Payment in advance reduces collection risk, but whether a Client could then reclaim fees already paid (enrichment, *par delictum*) is a question we did not research - UNVERIFIED. Severability clause should not be relied on to save the fee clause.

**R11 - E-signature: AES not needed (low).** No statute requires this agreement to be signed, so s13(1) does not apply; an ordinary e-signature under s13(3) is valid (*Spring Forest*). Name the e-signature method in the agreement and restrict variations to that method. Consumer Form 4 consent: Regulator accepts electronic "I give my consent" (GN 7.2.5); the attorney may wish to confirm Form 4's "signature" line does not trigger s13(1).

**R12 - NCC registry overrides consent (moderate, operational).** The NCC's stated position is that a pre-emptive block overrides earlier POPIA consent, and fresh consent may not cure it until the block is withdrawn. A consumer who registered a block and then submits a SortMyCover form may not lawfully receive electronic marketing calls from the Client. Both parties should register and cleanse; the agreement should allocate who checks the registry before first contact and what happens to a delivered lead that turns out to be blocked (suggest: replaced, counts as not delivered). No registration deadline announced as at 19 Aug 2026 - confirm current status.

**R13 - No-show proof photos (low).** A face-to-face proof photo "of the meeting location" may capture the consumer's home or third parties. Require proof that shows time and place type only, no persons, no address; Teams screenshot showing only the Client. Store proofs on the short retention schedule.

**R14 - Advertising: Client's GCoC s14 duty (low-moderate).** If SortMyCover ads are seen as advertising the Client's services, s14(2)(d) puts a duty on the Client to ensure or mitigate consistency. Give the Client view access and a withdrawal right; keep ads free of premiums (s14(3)(c)) and product claims.

**R15 - Citation hygiene.** Use [2026] ZAGPJHC 388 (SAFLII). LawLibrary number reported as 263 by Deneys - check. Judgment is marked not reportable and arose on an unopposed default application; commentators expect it may be distinguished. It is still the only judgment on point.
