## How each hook was judged (the gate, in order)
1. **2.1.8 personal attributes.** No second-person claim about the viewer's money, debt, family, age or health. A scene, a third-person fact, or a question about a scene is fine. "Your …" about cover or money is not.
2. **≤ 8 words of frame-1 text** (4.2). "2–4×" counts as one word.
3. **2.1.5 no fabricated statistics.** A hook that says "most", or gives a number about the world, ships only if its source is on file with compliance-qa. If it isn't, the brief uses the named fallback.
4. **Mechanism kept** (4D.4a "why it should work" column). My five decide this. **Loomer:** naming a known problem beats a curiosity gap in finance. **Harry Dry:** concrete beats abstract. **Meta docs:** text at 0.0 s, motion by 0.5 s, payoff promised in frame 1 and delivered by second 6.
5. **Cycle-1 production physics** (4D.5). Typographic and code-rendered, so no faces and no photography. A hook whose mechanism needs a face (the H3/H5/H7 POV note in 4D.4a) gets motion plus type instead. The face test waits for the week-3 Flow trigger.

**Rule for every row:** text is readable at 0.0 s. Motion starts between 0.2 and 0.5 s. The payoff is on screen by 6.0 s at the latest. Cuts land every 2–3 s. The end card shows "Sort your cover. 30 minutes. A real adviser." + "Tap to check your cover".
