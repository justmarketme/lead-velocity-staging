# Research: Meta ads facts for the broker onboarding film

Researched 6 Oct 2026. Primary sources are Meta Business Help Centre (BHC), Meta for Developers (Marketing API docs) and Meta's Transparency Centre. BHC pages don't show dates. Where a page has no date, I note the date I checked it. Anything published before 2024 is marked **[possibly stale]**.

---

## 0. Key restrictions that change what the film can say

1. **Lookalikes: the film may mention them only with conditions.** Ads for financial products and services, including insurance, can fall under Meta's "Financial products and services" Special Ad Category (SAC). Lookalike audiences and Advantage+ audience are unavailable inside that category. The SAC is **mandatory only** for advertisers based in the US or reaching US audiences (since 21 Jan 2025). The older credit rules also cover Canada and parts of Europe. A **South African advertiser reaching only South Africa** must fill in the SAC field, but Meta says it "can choose to opt-in or opt-out of audience restrictions". So today, lookalikes are **available** for SA-only life-cover campaigns set to `NONE`. They are gone if we opt into the SAC or ever target the US, Canada or Europe. Meta's own lookalike pages carry the banner "Lookalike audiences are limited or unavailable for some ads about financial products and services". So the film must not promise lookalikes as guaranteed or permanent.
2. **Since 2 Sep 2025 Meta blocks some custom and lookalike audiences.** It flags and blocks any custom or lookalike audience that suggests specific health conditions or financial status (examples it gives: "diabetes", "credit score", "high income"). For life cover, audience names, file headers and source segments must stay neutral. Don't use names like "smokers", "high-earners" or "chronic illness".
3. **Volume reality.** Meta's learning phase usually ends after about 50 optimisation events in the week after the last significant edit. One broker's ~37 raw leads a month is about 9 a week. A single-broker ad set optimising for Lead will usually show **"Learning limited"**. Conversions API for CRM ("conversion leads") wants at least 200 leads a month. The film must not suggest each broker's own ads "train the algorithm" quickly. Any learning comes from pooled, platform-level volume (Lead Velocity's account), and that is an architecture claim we have to make true before the film says it.
4. **Customer-list (hashed) uploads need a lawful basis.** Meta's Customer List Custom Audiences Terms require the advertiser to warrant "all necessary rights and permissions and a lawful basis". Under POPIA, only people who ticked our separate, optional ad-measurement consent may go into seed lists. Hashing doesn't remove the need for that basis.

## 2. Seed sources and "more data is better"

- **Customer list (hashed):** "name, email address, and phone number is hashed before you upload" ([Customer List Custom Audiences Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms/customaudience)). Not every row matches a Meta account, so the uploaded list needs to be bigger than 100 to produce 100 matched people. **The match rate is not published by Meta. Don't state a figure.**
- **Pixel + Conversions API (CAPI) events:** Lead (form submit), Schedule (booking), and an offline/CRM "Attended" event. CAPI sends events from our server, which can include hashed email and phone that the browser Pixel can't see.
- **Event Match Quality (EMQ):** Meta scores 0–10 "how effective the customer information parameters sent with your server event may be at matching events to Meta accounts". Better matching "can result in better ad performance and lower cost per action". EMQ covers website events sent via CAPI ([BHC: About Event Match Quality](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/765081237991954); [Dataset Quality API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/integration-quality-api/)).
- **Learning phase:** "ad sets exit the learning phase … usually … after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit". If an ad set is unlikely to get about 50 optimisation events in that week, it shows "Learning limited", which "isn't a penalty" ([BHC: About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700); [BHC: Learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981)). Significant edits (creative, audience, optimisation event, adding an ad, pausing) restart the count. The popular "20% budget" threshold is a third-party heuristic, not a Meta number.
- **Conversion leads / CAPI for CRM:** Meta's fit guidelines are: Instant Forms, "at least 200 leads per month", daily uploads, and the target stage within 28 days with a 1–40% conversion rate. Learning takes about 2–4 weeks, and total time to value is about 3–4 weeks ([Conversion Leads CRM Integration](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/conversion-leads-integration)). Meta's marketing page claims a 19% lower average cost per quality lead ([Meta: CAPI for CRM](https://www.facebook.com/business/generate-leads/conversions-api-for-crm)). That's Meta's own average; don't present it as our expected result. Note that conversion-leads optimisation only works with **Instant Forms**, not a website/WhatsApp funnel.
- **What "more data" concretely means for us:** (a) more consented, matchable people in the seed (100 is the minimum, 1,000+ is better); (b) more optimisation events per ad set per week (about 50 to exit learning); (c) richer identifiers per event (higher EMQ); (d) events that sit deeper in the funnel (Attended, not just Lead). Once the volume is there, (d) is the most valuable.

## 3. A/B testing on Meta

- **A/B test (Ads Manager / Experiments):** splits the audience "and ensure that nobody sees both". Meta recommends "the same budget for both versions" ([BHC: About A/B testing](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). Meta's best-practice page recommends one variable, at least 7 days, at most 30 days. It reports a winner with a confidence percentage and recommends ≥80% estimated power. (Wording via [AdTestBench, checked 26 Sep 2026](https://adtestbench.com/guides/creative-testing-framework/), which quotes BHC; the BHC best-practice page itself didn't render.)
- **Creative test (in-campaign):** "compare up to 7 creative variants" by making 2–7 copies of an ad. It needs the Highest-volume bid strategy. Meta suggests putting no more than ~20% of budget into it, and it reports **no confidence level** ([BHC: Set up a creative test](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1423851372208214)). The API version supports 2–5 cells ([Split Testing API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/guides/split-testing/)).
- **Practical rule for our volume:** test 2–3 very different concepts, not 7 small tweaks. At ~9 leads a week per broker, a fair test needs several weeks or pooled budget. Each variant ideally needs dozens of results before you pick a winner. (That last point is a practitioner heuristic, not Meta's.)
- **Creative fatigue:** Meta defines it as "an audience has seen the same creative too many times … higher cost per result" ([BHC: Creative fatigue recommendations](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1346816142327858)). The delivery statuses "Creative limited" and "Creative fatigue" are reportedly tied to cost per result rising, about 2x past ads for "fatigue". That threshold comes from secondary sources, not text I fetched from BHC. Meta publishes **no frequency threshold**. The figures of 2.5–3 a week are practitioner heuristics.

## 4. Timeline arithmetic (our numbers, simple division)

| Target | At 20/month (Bronze qualified) | At 37/month (raw leads) | At 45/month (top of range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 people | 5.0 months | ~2.7 months | ~2.2 months (~10 weeks) |
| 50 events (one learning-phase "week") | 2.5 months | ~1.4 months | ~1.1 months |
| Weekly rate vs ~50/week learning threshold | ~4.6/week (≈9% of it) | ~8.5/week (≈17%) | ~10.4/week (≈21%) |

Takeaways: one Bronze broker's own **attended/qualified** history takes about **5 months** to reach a 100-person lookalike seed. That's longer if not everyone consents to ad measurement or matches a Meta account. Divide by the consent rate: at 50% consent, 100 seeds take ~10 months at 20/month. **No single broker's ad set will exit the learning phase on its own volume.**

## 5. Things the film must NOT claim

- Any guaranteed number of leads, appointments, attendances, policies or commission. FAIS General Code Part X: no exaggerated expectations, give a balanced presentation, include key limitations ([FAIS Part X](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-advisory-and-intermediary-services-act-2002/bn80_part_x_advertising_and_direct_marketing)).
- That we target people by health, income, credit score, family circumstances or any other sensitive trait. Meta blocks audiences that suggest health or financial status (Sep 2025) and its discrimination policy forbids it.
- That lookalikes are permanently available or guaranteed. They are unavailable under the Financial products and services SAC, and Meta says they're "limited or unavailable for some" financial ads.
- That "Meta's AI finds your perfect clients" or that results are predictable. Advantage+ treats inputs as suggestions and may deliver outside them.
- That a broker's own small volume trains the algorithm quickly (see §4).
- Meta's own averages (e.g. "19% lower cost per quality lead") stated as our results.
- That hashing makes uploading a customer list consent-free. POPIA s11 still needs a lawful basis. Meta's terms require "a lawful basis". Seed lists only include people who opted into our separate ad-measurement consent. Under the Information Regulator's Guidance Note on Direct Marketing (3 Dec 2024), WhatsApp/DM/phone marketing to non-customers needs prior opt-in consent (s69), and silence isn't consent ([Information Regulator Guidance Note](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf); [CDH summary, Jan 2025](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2025/Sectors/Technology-Communications/Technology-and-Communications-Alert-29-january-Navigating-the-Information-Regulators-guidance-note-on-direct-marketing)).
- Any specific EMQ score, match rate or learning-phase duration in days as a promise. Meta says learning usually takes at least a week, and it can be "Learning limited" indefinitely.

## Plain-English facts the film may state

1. "Meta needs at least 100 people from one country to build a lookalike audience, and it works better with a thousand or more." [Meta Marketing API: Lookalike Audiences; Meta BHC as quoted 2020 for the 1,000+ guidance]
2. "A lookalike audience finds new people who share characteristics with a group you already know, like clients who actually attended a meeting." [Meta BHC: About lookalike audiences]
3. "Quality beats quantity: a list of people who attended is a better starting point than a list of everyone who clicked." [Meta BHC, "Source quality matters too", as quoted 2020; Marketing API conversion-seed guidance]
4. "In South Africa, life-cover ads can currently use lookalikes. That can change, and in some countries Meta switches them off for financial products entirely." [Meta Marketing API: Special Ad Categories; Meta BHC lookalike banner]
5. "Meta usually needs about fifty results in a week before an ad set settles. Below that, it keeps learning, and that's normal." [Meta BHC: About the learning phase; Learning limited]
6. "Every big change, like a new ad, a new audience or a new goal, restarts that learning, so we change things on purpose, not on impulse." [Meta BHC: About the learning phase]
7. "Telling Meta who actually attended, not just who filled in a form, helps it look for more people like them." [Meta Marketing API: Conversion Leads CRM Integration; BHC: Event Match Quality]
8. "Meta scores how well our event data matches real accounts from zero to ten, and better matching can lower the cost of each result." [Meta BHC: About Event Match Quality]
9. "We test a few genuinely different ads side by side for at least a week before choosing a winner." [Meta BHC: About A/B testing / A/B best practices]
10. "When people have seen the same ad too often, results get more expensive, so fresh creative is part of the plan." [Meta BHC: Creative fatigue recommendations]
11. "At around twenty qualified people a month, building a group of a hundred takes about five months. This is a slow build, not a switch." [Our arithmetic: 100 ÷ 20]
12. "We only use someone's details for ad measurement if they ticked that separate box, and we never target people by health, income or other sensitive traits." [POPIA s11/s69 + Information Regulator Guidance Note (Dec 2024); Meta Customer List Custom Audiences Terms; Meta Sep 2025 audience flagging]
