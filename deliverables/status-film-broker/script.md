# Where we are: the broker film (shareable with any broker)

Voice: en-US-AndrewNeural (house explainer voice), rate -3%. About 3 minutes 30 seconds (estimate from the word count; not yet measured because the voice service is unavailable). Same house design as the onboarding film and the internal status film (pink to purple gradient, white cards, navy text).
Audience: any broker, including prospects, who receives this video on WhatsApp. Say "we" (Lead Velocity) and "you" (the broker). No person, client, company-of-a-client or competitor is named. No fee amounts (the pricing page is the source). One status date only, in the scene 5 narration.
Status wording: built, in final set-up, still being finished, not switched on yet. Lead delivery has not started. Scenes 5 and 6 go out of date as the system is finished, and so do the "runs the ads" line in scene 1 and the "once the Microsoft sign-in is switched on" line in scene 7: re-cut all four when the system is live. Scenes 3 and 4 state the terms of the lead generation agreement v0.2, which is still a draft for practitioner review: re-check scenes 3 and 4 when v0.2 is approved, and have the founder confirm that approval before the first send.
Every factual sentence is traced to a file in `sources.md`.

## Scene 1: What Lead Velocity does for you (about 20s)
**Say:** Lead Velocity books calls into your diary, with people who have asked to speak to an adviser. You pay one flat fee per thirty-day cycle. It's never per policy, and never commission. Our consumer brand, SortMyCover, publishes honest, broker-neutral content and runs the ads. We never give advice. That part is yours.
**Show:** Lead Velocity logo. Title "Where we are". Four-node flow draws left to right: SortMyCover ads and content → WhatsApp quick questions → Your diary (Teams meeting) → You, the adviser. Chips: Flat fee per 30-day cycle · No commission · We give no advice.

## Scene 2: How a lead reaches you (about 30s)
**Say:** Here's how leads will reach you once you're switched on. Someone taps a SortMyCover ad or post. They agree to be contacted, we verify their WhatsApp number, and they answer a few quick questions. Higher-budget leads then pick a time from the free slots in your calendar. A Teams meeting lands in your diary, and they get your intro card, with your name, practice and FSP number. We then send a one-tap link to confirm their email.
**Show:** Opening caption "How a lead will reach you once you're switched on". Left to right journey that ticks itself: Ad or post → Website or WhatsApp → Consent + WhatsApp number verified → Quick questions → They pick a time (small label: "A-tier now · B-tier after you accept") → Teams meeting + intro card → Email link to confirm. Phone mock on the right: WhatsApp chat receiving an illustrative intro card (placeholders only: "Your name", "Your practice", "FSP your number", "Teams call, 30 minutes"). Caption under the phone: "Illustration".

## Scene 3: A-tier and B-tier leads (about 35s)
**Say:** Every lead is labelled A-tier or B-tier, by the monthly budget they tell us. It's their own answer, which we don't check. Leads in A-tier say fifteen hundred rand or more, and count automatically. Leads in B-tier say seven hundred and fifty rand to fourteen hundred and ninety-nine rand. You see a short summary, and accept or decline within two working hours. If you accept, they pick a time. If you decline or miss the window, it doesn't count and you're not charged. Under seven hundred and fifty rand, we filter the lead out.
**Show:** Three stacked bands. A-tier "R1,500 or more a month · counts automatically" (green). B-tier "R750 to R1,499 a month · you accept or decline" (amber), with a phone card "New enquiry · B-tier · age band · budget band · reason for the call" and two buttons Accept / Decline, a note "Accept → they pick a time", plus a 2 working hours timer ring. Under R750 "Filtered out · never sent, never counted" (grey). Footnote: "Budget is the person's own answer."

## Scene 4: What you can rely on (about 35s)
**Say:** Wrong contact details never count. If someone doesn't turn up, we may replace the lead as a goodwill gesture, when you send proof, up to a weekly limit. If delivery runs late, anything still owed rolls into your next cycle, on top, or you can ask for a refund per lead. If you question a lead, ask in writing, and within two working days we send the consent record, booking confirmation and their own answers. We don't promise attendance or sales. The full terms are in the agreement you sign.
**Show:** Four white tiles that tick in turn: Wrong contact details never count · No-show replacement (goodwill, you send proof, weekly limit) · Late delivery rolls over on top, or a refund per lead · Dispute evidence in 2 working days (consent record · booking confirmation · their own answers). Closing lines fade in: "No promises of attendance or sales." then "The full terms are in the agreement you sign."

## Scene 7: Your onboarding in six steps (about 35s)
**Say:** After a short welcome video, onboarding is six steps in the portal. First, your details and FSP number, checked on the FSCA register. Next, connect your Outlook calendar with one tap, once it's switched on. We only look at when you're free, and we add your meetings. We never read your emails. Then check your working hours, sign your agreement, and approve your intro card. Last, record an optional twenty-five second intro. When onboarding is done and your first payment has cleared, we run a final check and switch you on.
**Show:** Seven step cards in portal style, numbered 1 to 7 as in the portal, each highlighting as it is spoken: 1 Watch the 3-minute video (muted, tag "Optional") · 2 Your details and FSP number · 3 Connect your Outlook calendar (tag "Once Microsoft sign-in is switched on"; text "We only look at when you are free, and we add your meetings. We never read your emails.") · 4 Your hours and how you meet · 5 Sign your agreement · 6 Approve your intro card · 7 Record your 25-second intro (tag "Optional").
