# SortMyCover — angle ranking for cycle 1 (affordability + active intent)

**Owner:** creative-strategist · **Date:** 2026-10-03 · **Status:** PROPOSAL. The money split is Jonathan's (NH-64). Nothing here is published or uploaded.
**Direction (from Jonathan):** find clients who would easily pay for life cover, who are looking for it now, from several angles.

## How to read the scores
- Scores are 1–5 and are **my judgment from the creative, not data.** They get replaced by CPL, reply rate and booking rate once ≥ 30 leads per arm exist (4.4 "cycle 1 reality"). At about R300 a day that will not happen for most arms in cycle 1.
- **AFFORDABILITY:** how likely the person this ad pulls clears the R750–R1,250+ budget band easily (salaried professionals, homeowners, established 40-somethings, business owners).
- **ACTIVE INTENT:** how likely they are already thinking about cover now (a trigger moment, a policy up for review, or a stated worry about checking).
- Ads stay third person (2.1.8). The ad cannot target by income. "Who it speaks to" is the only lever, so a scene that only a homeowner or salaried person recognises is the affordability filter.

## Confirm or argue against

**Confirmed:**
1. **The set of 6.** It covers five distinct angles (employer gap, new bond, new baby, turned 40, bond paperwork), which meets the Andromeda "distinct, not variants" rule, and it fits the 4–6 concepts of cycle 1 in 4.4.
2. **Hold C06 and C12.** C06 sits at the bottom on both signals and is the one theme 2.1.8 warns about. C12 is compliance-cleared but ranks low on affordability. Neither is worth a slot while four stronger ideas wait in the pool.
3. **C14 as the placeholder for C16.** It has already been through compliance-qa, so the slot never sits empty.
4. **C17 first in the pool.** It is the only new angle that reaches people who already pay for cover.

**Argued against, or flagged:**
1. **The colour test cannot be read in cycle 1.** Amber against teal needs about 30 leads on each arm, and the whole cycle gives about 40. Meta will probably also skew delivery to one of two near-identical ads, which is the Andromeda "variants of one angle" problem. I keep it because both versions are already rendered and compliance-checked, and it costs a slot. My advice is to treat it as **one concept in two colours**, not two concepts, and to read it only from CPL after cycle 1. If Jonathan wants more reach for the money, give that slot to C17 once it clears.
2. **C03 and C16 are close cousins.** Both are about the bond. They differ in scene (checklist against paperwork) and in the moment (approval against signing), but if the first week shows them cannibalising each other, drop C03 from the set and keep whichever has the lower CPL.
3. **Refresh pool order.** Jonathan's pool has C08 and C10. I would put **C11 ahead of C10** (business owners rank higher on affordability) and C08 last (format benefit only).
4. **C02 is in nobody's list.** It is the second-best salaried concept, with the same angle as C01. Keep it as a reserve for when C01 fatigues, ahead of C08.
5. **Intent is the better lever than affordability.** Affordability can only be hinted at through the scene. Trigger and review moments are where the ad can really select. That favours C03, C16, C17 and C04 over any "who you are" angle.
